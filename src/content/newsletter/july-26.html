Plan A
Larsen, Dean, Halstead, Lifland, Greenblatt, and Kokotajlo, July 2026

  • A mix between a forecast and a policy prescription on how the U.S. will/should address powerful AI systems. Plenty has been discussed on this so I won't add too much. Like others, I dislike the muddied distinction between normative policy recommendations and predicted unfortunate actions. I also think they overplay job loss (cf. Alex Imas) and single-agent theory. Finally, I'm skeptical about slowing progress by limiting algorithmic improvements; theory/algorithm improvements are exactly what will be accelerated most by powerful AI (cf. "Human mathematicians are being out counter-exampled") and it's hard to know a-priori the impact of fundamental research. Alignment research can advance capabilities (e.g., RLHF) and theoretical research (e.g., learning mechanics) could as easily advance capabilities as improve alignment.
